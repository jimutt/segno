## Context

DL-005 settled that the built-in controls appear only with the `controls` attribute, as with `<audio>`, and that a site may bring its own controls in a slot.

## Decision

The built-in controls are a single bar of native form elements:

- a play/pause `<button>` (part `play-button`) with an icon and an accessible name that follows the state;
- the time, "0:12 / 0:28" (part `time`);
- a seek `<input type="range">` over the whole audio (part `seek`), with an accessible value text ("0:12 of 0:28");
- a tempo `<select>`, 50–150% in steps of 10%, setting `playbackRate` with pitch preserved (part `tempo`).

They render as the fallback content of `<slot name="controls">`, shown only when the host has `controls`. A site's own controls go in that slot and call the element's API. The bar wraps onto two lines in a player narrower than 28rem (size container query), and `--segno-controls: compact` shows only the play button and the seek slider, on one line (DL-015). The controls area is sticky at the bottom of the viewport, so the controls stay in reach while the pages scroll.

**Keyboard**, on the focusable pages area: Space or K plays and pauses, ← and → move to the previous and next bar, Home goes to the start. Without markers, ← and → move five seconds instead. Keys with Alt, Ctrl or Meta are left to the browser, a held key doesn't toggle playback repeatedly, and the shortcuts are listed in `aria-keyshortcuts`. The native controls keep their own keyboard handling. The current bar is announced politely to screen readers when playback pauses or after a seek, not during continuous playback, where an announcement per bar would drown everything else.

## Rationale

Native elements bring keyboard, focus and assistive-technology support for free and are easy to theme. One bar with four elements covers what a listener needs; anything more belongs to the site.

## Consequences

- A site that wants other controls (loop, follow toggle, download) builds them in the slot with the public API.
